# Systems Thinking

## Definition

Systems thinking is seeing how people, processes, tools, and incentives influence one another over time — so a learner solves the **root cause**, not the visible symptom, and anticipates side effects.

## Why it matters here

Real problems are rarely isolated. Lateness, food waste, failing small businesses, and traffic are system behaviors. A solution that ignores the system often moves the problem somewhere else or creates a new one.

## The model — Map before you build

1. **Observe** the situation end to end.
2. **Map** the parts: actors, inputs, outputs, steps.
3. **Connect** — what depends on what?
4. **Find root causes** — keep asking "why?" past the first answer.
5. **Find leverage points** — the small change with a big effect.
6. **Predict consequences** — could the fix cause a new problem?
7. **Build, measure, improve.**

## Common misconceptions

- *"The symptom is the problem."* Late students may be a scheduling, transport, or motivation issue — not an app-shaped one.
- *"More features = better solution."* Often the leverage point is a single small change.
- *"My solution has no side effects."* Every intervention perturbs the system.

## Tutor moves

- "Who else is involved?" / "What happens just before and just after this step?"
- "What depends on what here?"
- "What's the root cause, not the first cause?"
- "What small change could create a big improvement?"
- "Could your solution create a new problem for someone?"

## Mission hooks

Before building any solution, learners fill a lightweight stakeholder + flow map: primary users, secondary users, decision-makers, inputs, outputs, constraints, and possible consequences. Advanced missions (Innovator) require identifying at least one leverage point and one predicted side effect.

## Mastery rubric

| Level | What it looks like |
|------|--------------------|
| Awareness | Lists the visible parts of a system when prompted. |
| Application | Explains relationships and identifies a plausible root cause with guidance. |
| Independence | Finds leverage points and predicts consequences for a new problem unaided. |
| Leadership | Guides others to map systems and question root causes. |

## Assessment signals

Depth of root-cause reasoning; whether stakeholders beyond the obvious were named; whether side effects were predicted and checked.
