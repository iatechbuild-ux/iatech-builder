# Critical Thinking

## Definition

Critical thinking is understanding a problem deeply enough to **justify, test, and improve** a decision — and to detect when an answer (including an AI's answer) is weak.

## Why it matters here

AI produces fluent, confident, often-wrong output. The learner's edge is judgment: the ability to ask whether a claim is true, whose interest it serves, and what evidence supports it. Critical thinking is what turns "the AI told me" into "I checked, and here's what it missed."

## The model — the Question Ladder

Learners climb from surface to depth:
1. **Observe** — what is actually happening?
2. **Question** — what am I assuming? what would change my mind?
3. **Evidence** — what supports or contradicts this?
4. **Compare** — what are the alternatives, and their trade-offs?
5. **Decide** — choose, and record *why*.
6. **Test** — does reality agree?
7. **Revise** — update the decision when evidence changes.

## Common misconceptions

- *"Critical thinking means disagreeing / being negative."* No — it means proportioning belief to evidence, which sometimes means agreeing.
- *"If the AI is confident, it's right."* Confidence is a writing style, not a truth signal.
- *"There is one correct answer to find."* Most real problems have trade-offs, not a single key.

## Tutor moves

Ask, and wait:
- "What do you notice?" → "What makes you think that?"
- "What evidence do you have?" / "What would change your mind?"
- "What else could be true?"
- "What did the AI miss or get wrong?"
- Resist giving the answer; reflect the question back.

## Mission hooks

- Mission opens: *"A school buys an attendance app to fix lateness."* Thinking challenge: **will an app actually fix lateness, or is the cause something else?** Learners must justify before building.
- Every AI-practice step asks the learner to name one thing the AI got wrong or incomplete.

## Mastery rubric

| Level | What it looks like |
|------|--------------------|
| Awareness | Notices problems and asks simple questions when prompted. |
| Application | Compares two options and explains a choice with guidance. |
| Independence | Evaluates evidence, tests an assumption, and revises a decision unprompted. |
| Leadership | Coaches another learner to examine assumptions and evidence. |

## Assessment signals

Quality of problem framing; whether decisions cite evidence; whether the learner caught an AI error; improvement between versions.
