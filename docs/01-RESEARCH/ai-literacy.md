# AI Literacy

## Definition

AI literacy is understanding what AI can and cannot do, using it responsibly and safely, and — most importantly — **verifying its output** instead of trusting it.

## Why it matters here

This is the competency the whole "external AI practice" strategy exists to build. IATECH's position: **AI is a collaborator, not an authority.** A learner who can use AI *and* catch its mistakes is exactly what the AI era rewards.

## What learners should understand

- What AI is, at a high level, and what generative AI does.
- Why it hallucinates and makes confident errors.
- Bias and fairness in AI output.
- Privacy and safety: what must never be shared with an AI tool.
- How to verify a claim the AI made.
- When *not* to use AI at all.

## Safety rule (non-negotiable, taught every time)

Never share with an AI tool: passwords, home address, phone numbers, private family information, school-sensitive data, or confidential documents. Reinforced in [`60-OPERATIONS/student-handbook.md`](../60-OPERATIONS/student-handbook.md) and [`60-OPERATIONS/safeguarding-and-data-policy.md`](../60-OPERATIONS/safeguarding-and-data-policy.md).

## Common misconceptions

- *"The AI knows things / is always right."* It predicts plausible text; plausibility ≠ truth.
- *"If it sounds confident, it's correct."* Confidence is style.
- *"Using AI is cheating."* Using it *and hiding it* is; using it *and verifying and explaining it* is the skill.

## Tutor moves

- "What did you ask, and what did it give you?"
- "What in this answer would you bet is wrong? How could you check?"
- "What did the AI miss that you know from the real situation?"
- "Was there private information in your prompt? Should there have been?"

## Mission hooks

Every AI-practice step requires the learner to submit: the prompt, a summary of the output, **one thing that was wrong or incomplete**, and how they verified or improved it. This is the heart of [`10-PRODUCT/ai-practice-verification.md`](../10-PRODUCT/ai-practice-verification.md) — and it needs no paid API.

## Mastery rubric

| Level | What it looks like |
|------|--------------------|
| Awareness | Explains what AI is for and follows safety rules when reminded. |
| Application | Uses AI on a task and, with guidance, spots an error. |
| Independence | Independently verifies AI output and decides when not to use it. |
| Leadership | Teaches peers to use AI safely and to verify its claims. |

## Assessment signals

Whether the learner caught a real AI error; quality of verification; adherence to the safety rule; ability to explain when AI was the wrong tool.
