# Product Design Principles

The UX rules that turn the philosophy into screens. When a design decision is unclear, these break the tie.

## The principles

1. **A learner always knows the next step.** Never leave a learner facing a screen without an obvious primary action.
2. **One primary job per screen.** One dominant call-to-action; everything else is secondary.
3. **Mission language, not course language.** "Start mission," "Ship it," "Submit your build" — not "Module 3, Unit 2."
4. **Show progress; make completion feel earned.** Progress is visible; finishing a mission is celebrated without turning into a slot machine.
5. **Tutors never leave the app for lesson notes.** The facilitation guide lives beside the lesson.
6. **Parents see outcomes, not telemetry.** Projects, plain-language skills, tutor comments — no raw metrics.
7. **Admins manage quality, not chase data.** Content and safety actions are direct and few-click.
8. **Reduce confusion, increase building time.** Every removed distraction is a feature.
9. **Warm, simple, confident copy.** Short sentences. Encouraging, never condescending.
10. **Design for the phone and the weak signal first.** If it works there, it works everywhere.
11. **Honesty over delight when they conflict.** An honest "saved on your device, not yet uploaded" beats a fake success checkmark.

## Per-surface intent

- **Student dashboard:** current mission + Continue, progress, badges, portfolio preview. Nothing else competes.
- **Tutor dashboard:** today's lesson, attendance, who's stuck, review queue.
- **Parent dashboard:** projects completed, skill progress (plain language), tutor comments, attendance, next milestone.
- **Admin dashboard:** content management, cohorts, safety flags, reports.

## The tie-breaker question

If two designs are otherwise equal, choose the one that a first-time computer user on a small screen can complete without help.
