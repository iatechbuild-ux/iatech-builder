# Graduate Profile

## Identity

An IATECH graduate **solves problems and thinks differently.** They are not "a coder." They are a problem solver who understands that technology serves people, and who happens to be able to build software.

## Demonstrable capabilities

By the end of a full learning journey, a graduate can — and can *show evidence that they can*:

| Capability | Evidence it produces |
|---|---|
| Identify a meaningful problem | A problem statement naming who is affected and why it matters |
| Understand users and stakeholders | A short stakeholder map or user note |
| Choose the right tool for the job | A decision log explaining why this approach over alternatives |
| Build websites and simple software | Runnable artifacts in the portfolio |
| Write foundational Python | A working script that solves a real task |
| Use AI responsibly and critically | An AI-practice transcript plus a reflection on what the AI got wrong |
| Deploy a project | A live public URL |
| Present and defend a solution | A recorded or in-person explanation |
| Build a portfolio | The portfolio itself, tagged by competency |
| Collaborate and lead | Peer/mentor evidence (later pathways) |
| Keep learning independently | A self-directed extension challenge completed without step-by-step guidance |

## The distinguishing trait

The graduate can look at a messy real situation, find the actual problem underneath the obvious symptom, and ship something that measurably helps. That trait — not any specific language or framework — is what IATECH certifies through portfolio evidence.

## Anti-profile (what we are *not* producing)

- A learner who can pass a quiz but cannot build.
- A learner who ships AI output they cannot explain or evaluate.
- A learner who knows syntax but cannot say whose problem it solves.
