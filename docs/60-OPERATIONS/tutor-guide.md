# Tutor Guide

## Your role

You are a **facilitator, not a lecturer, and not an answer key.** The software carries the curriculum so you can carry the judgment, motivation, and relationship. Your job is to help learners *think*, not to hand them solutions.

## The core move: ask, then wait

Your most powerful tool is a good question followed by silence. Default questions:
- What problem are we solving, and who has it?
- What do you notice? What makes you think that?
- What have you tried? What's your evidence?
- What did the AI get wrong or miss — and how do you know?
- How could you improve it? How will you test that it helped?

## Before class

- Open today's mission and lesson; the **facilitation guide is in the app** — you never hunt for notes.
- Review the lesson objective and the thinking challenge.
- Check the review queue for pending submissions.

## During class

- Start from the real-world problem, not the code.
- Keep learners *building*; step in with a question, not an answer, when they stall.
- Help the stuck without solving it for them ("what's the smallest next step?").

## After class

- Review submissions against the rubric; leave **specific, actionable, encouraging** feedback.
- Mark attendance; note who needs support next time.

## Reviewing AI practice (important)

For each submission, check the AI-practice evidence: does the learner name something the AI got *wrong or incomplete*, and how they verified it? A hollow answer ("it was helpful") earns a revision request — this is where the whole AI-literacy goal lives. See [`../10-PRODUCT/ai-practice-verification.md`](../10-PRODUCT/ai-practice-verification.md).

## Good feedback looks like

> "Strong start — your page has clear sections. The submit button is hard to see on a phone; improve the contrast and test it again on mobile. Also, you spotted that the AI's layout ignored small screens — good catch."

Specific · encouraging · actionable · improvement-focused. Revision is part of building, never a punishment.

## Safeguarding (read the policy)

If a learner's reflection or submission suggests they are unsafe or at risk, **raise a safety flag immediately** — it routes straight to an admin, outside the normal queue. Do not attempt to handle a safeguarding concern alone. See [`safeguarding-and-data-policy.md`](safeguarding-and-data-policy.md).

## What to avoid

Solving everything for learners · over-lecturing · fixating on syntax · skipping reflection · rubber-stamping AI-pasted work.
