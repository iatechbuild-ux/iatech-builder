# Assessment System v3

## Two decisions, not one score

Tutors make two separate decisions:

1. **Artifact decision:** Is this submission safe, functional, and fit for its stated purpose?
2. **Competency decision:** What can the learner demonstrably do, under what assistance conditions?

The weighted project score supports feedback but cannot independently award mastery.

## Universal rubric

Score each row 0–4 using the behavioral mastery levels. Mark **Not assessed** when the mission does not provide valid evidence for a row; do not convert it to zero.

| Dimension | Core question |
|---|---|
| Problem and stakeholder understanding | Did the learner diagnose the real need and affected people? |
| Requirements and tool choice | Are success criteria clear, and is the chosen tool justified? |
| Technical/domain quality | Does the artifact demonstrate the named domain competencies correctly? |
| Testing, safety, and responsibility | Did the learner test normal, edge, privacy, accessibility, and failure conditions appropriate to the domain? |
| AI judgment | Did the learner disclose assistance, verify output, and retain decision ownership? |
| Revision and reflection | Did evidence and critique produce a meaningful improvement and accurate reflection? |
| Communication | Can the learner explain the result, choices, limitations, and next steps? |

Domain rubrics add observable checks to “Technical/domain quality” and “Testing, safety, and responsibility”; they do not invent a separate grading philosophy.

## Critical-fail conditions

A submission must be revised regardless of average score when any applicable condition is true:

- the artifact does not perform its primary requirement;
- unsafe or unnecessary personal data is exposed;
- the learner cannot explain a critical part of the submitted work;
- required evidence is fabricated, inaccessible, or belongs to someone else;
- a website cannot be operated by keyboard or has unlabeled essential controls;
- an analysis makes a decision claim unsupported by its data;
- an automation design omits a material safety or failure condition;
- AI-generated work is presented without disclosure.

## Mastery evidence thresholds

### Awareness

- One formative check or guided artifact.
- Learner recognizes and explains the concept.
- No course-completion claim is based on Awareness alone.

### Application

- One approved contextual artifact.
- Assistance band A or B is permitted and recorded.
- Applicable domain checks score at least 2.

### Independence

- Two approved pieces of evidence in different contexts.
- At least one is a transfer task at assistance band C.
- No critical-fail condition remains.
- Applicable domain checks score at least 3 on the transfer task.

### Leadership

- Independence is already established.
- A tutor witnesses the learner reviewing, teaching, documenting, or facilitating another person's improvement.
- The helped learner or audience can use the explanation successfully.

## Competency evidence record

Every mastery update must record:

- competency code;
- claimed level;
- submission/evidence reference;
- mission context;
- assistance band and AI use;
- rubric rows used;
- tutor decision and date;
- transfer-context reference for Independence; and
- witness/beneficiary for Leadership.

## Domain release checks

| Domain | Non-negotiable checks |
|---|---|
| Web | Semantic structure, keyboard use, labels, responsive layout, primary flow, deployment |
| Python | Correct output, invalid input, decomposition, representative tests, readable explanation |
| Data | Reproducible cleaning, calculation accuracy, appropriate chart, limitations, privacy |
| CMS | Working build, content ownership, form/privacy, accessibility, maintenance/handover |
| Automation | Simulation, normal/edge/failure tests, safety behavior, logic explanation |
| Process analysis | Evidence-backed current state, root cause, requirements, measurable future state |
| AI | Disclosure, privacy, verification, bias/assumption check, human decision ownership |

## Tutor calibration

Before a course runs, curriculum operations must provide three anonymized examples for each capstone:

- **Not yet:** attractive or complete-looking work that fails an essential requirement.
- **Application:** correct work completed with meaningful guidance.
- **Independence:** correct transfer work with justified decisions and minimal support.

Tutors score the same examples and reconcile disagreements before reviewing learners.

## Reassessment

Revision requests identify the unmet requirement, evidence of the gap, and the smallest next action. Learners resubmit the affected evidence rather than restarting the entire mission. Revision history remains visible and improvement itself becomes evidence.
