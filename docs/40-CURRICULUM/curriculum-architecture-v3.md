# Curriculum Architecture v3

## Curriculum promise

IATECH develops learners who can diagnose meaningful problems and create responsible, tested, explainable technology solutions. Courses are capability pathways, not topic playlists.

## The curriculum stack

| Layer | Meaning | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Capability | Durable way of acting: Think, Understand, Build, Improve, Lead | Evidence accumulated across courses |
| Domain | Professional field such as web development or data analysis | Domain capstone |
| Skill | A teachable cluster such as responsive layout or data cleaning | Work across at least two contexts |
| Competency | Observable performance such as “labels every form control” | Assessed evidence item |
| Mission | Authentic problem that integrates several competencies | Reviewed artifact and explanation |
| Lesson/activity | Just-in-time instruction and deliberate practice | Formative check; never mastery by itself |

## Backward-design rule

Every course is designed in this order:

1. Define the independent exit performance.
2. Define acceptable evidence and failure conditions.
3. Identify prerequisite and target competencies.
4. Sequence missions from guided application to independent transfer.
5. Add only the lessons and practice needed for those missions.

## Course sequence

Each domain uses four instructional phases. These phases sit above the Experience → Understand → Rebuild → Master → Teach mission stages.

| Phase | Support | Purpose | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| On-ramp | High | Establish safety, vocabulary, tool confidence, and prerequisite concepts | Diagnostic and a small guided artifact |
| Core practice | Medium | Build correct mental models and reliable procedures | Two targeted practice artifacts |
| Integrated mission | Fading | Combine skills for a real user and revise after critique | Working artifact, tests, decision log, revision |
| Transfer capstone | Low | Solve a new problem and defend choices independently | New-context artifact, presentation, AI disclosure |

## Pathway expectations

### Explorer

- Designed for Awareness → Application.
- Short missions of 3–6 hours, normally one major new concept at a time.
- Worked examples, checklists, vocabulary help, and tutor prompts are visible.
- Exit requires Application in digital confidence, problem framing, AI safety/verification, one build domain, reflection, and explanation.

### Builder

- Designed for Application → Independence.
- Missions of 6–12 hours that combine multiple skills and require testing and revision.
- Hints are available on request and AI use must be logged.
- Exit requires independent transfer in problem solving and at least two build domains, including one deployed or externally usable artifact.

### Innovator

- Designed for Independence → Leadership.
- Missions of 12–24 hours with ambiguous requirements, stakeholder feedback, trade-offs, and a public product.
- Learners mentor, review, or document for others.
- Exit requires Independence across the common core and witnessed Leadership in at least one domain.

## Common core across every course

Every course assesses these recurring competencies in its own context:

1. Problem and stakeholder definition
2. Requirements and success criteria
3. Safe, private, and responsible technology use
4. AI disclosure and output verification
5. Testing and evidence-based revision
6. Clear explanation and reflection

These are spiralled. They are not repeated as identical lessons.

## Evidence rule

A competency claim requires all of the following:

- direct evidence tied to a named requirement;
- tutor review or tutor attestation;
- the conditions of performance recorded, including assistance used;
- success on the domain-specific rubric row; and
- for Independence, a second context not copied from guided practice.

One artifact may evidence several competencies, but a generic project score cannot silently raise unrelated competencies.

## Assistance bands

| Band | Permitted help | Maximum mastery claim |
|---|---|---|
| A — Modelled | Learner follows or modifies a worked example | Awareness |
| B — Guided | Tutor/AI provides steps, prompts, or frequent correction | Application |
| C — Consultative | Learner plans and builds; help answers specific questions | Independence, if transfer is demonstrated |
| D — Leadership | Learner independently builds and helps another learner improve | Leadership, with witnessed evidence |

The submission records the highest assistance actually used, not the assistance intended by the mission.

## Course completion rule

A course is complete only when:

- every essential exit competency is at or above the course target;
- the transfer capstone is approved;
- no critical safety, privacy, accessibility, or correctness defect remains;
- at least one critique was applied in a recorded revision;
- AI use is disclosed and verified; and
- the learner explains the artifact and its limits.

An average score cannot compensate for a failed critical requirement.

## Prerequisite rule

Mission prerequisites represent abilities needed before starting. Competencies taught by the mission are targets and must never also be prerequisites. Explorer entry missions should normally have no domain prerequisite beyond the digital-confidence on-ramp.

## Delivery assumptions

- Reference group: ages 10–18, placed by demonstrated skill.
- Reference device: low-cost Android phone; laptop access is scheduled when a build genuinely requires it.
- Delivery: tutor-guided cohort with asynchronous practice.
- A curriculum hour means approximately 45–60 minutes of active learner work.
- Time estimates are planning ranges, not promotion criteria.

## Minimum viable programme

The first credible programme should not claim equal depth in all 11 domains. It should ship as:

- Common core: digital confidence, thinking/problem solving, AI literacy/verification, process analysis, communication.
- Primary build routes: Web Development and Python Programming.
- Applied studios: Data Analysis, CMS/No-Code, and Automation/Robotics.
- Embedded practices: Product/Design, AI-Assisted Development, and Leadership.

This produces honest depth while retaining exposure to the wider Future Builder framework.
